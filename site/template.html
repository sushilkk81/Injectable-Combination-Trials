How the list is built

  • Sources: ClinicalTrials.gov (API v2) and the EU Clinical Trials Information System (CTIS). Both are re-queried every Monday.
  • Phase 2 or 3, including Phase 1/2 and 2/3 designs; recruiting, not yet recruiting, enrolling by invitation, or active and not recruiting.
  • At least one drug or biological given by a parenteral route, with the delivery device named in the record: title, interventions, arms or summary on ClinicalTrials.gov, or the product's pharmaceutical form and device fields in CTIS.
  • EU-registered trials are matched to their NCT number where one exists. Trials whose device appears only in the EU record are marked "Device listed in EU register".
  • Europe means EU/EEA countries plus the UK and Switzerland. Other European sites (for example Serbia or Georgia) count toward the total but not toward the Europe filter.

Revenue figures

  • Shown only for industry-sponsored Phase 3 and 2/3 trials, at asset level (all indications), in US$ billions.
  • Peak-sales forecast: the published estimate of peak annual worldwide sales for an investigational asset, with its source and date.
  • Marketed product sales: the company's latest reported annual sales for an approved product being studied in a new indication or presentation. Regional figures are the company's own where it reports them.
  • Blank means no credible public figure was found. Forecasts are third-party estimates, not company guidance, unless stated.

Limits to keep in mind

  • Many sponsors do not state the presentation in the registry, so trials that use a device without saying so are missing. Treat the list as a floor.
  • Device class comes from the wording of the record, not from the product's regulatory designation.
  • Refreshes are automatic. New trials are classified by the same rules but are not reviewed by hand until the next manual review.
  • Titles, drug names and country lists are shortened. Open the registry link for the full record.
